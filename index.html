Starting a goal with a burst of excitement, then losing interest before it is finished — a new planner on Monday, an empty one by Friday. Learn why it happens, then use the planner to keep going.

Stage 01

The Spark

A new goal feels exciting, and it’s easy to imagine how much you’ll get done.

Signs to look for
You make big promises or spend more time setting up than doing the first task.
Example scenario
After getting a low quiz score, Vince buys a new planner and fills it with two-hour review blocks every night.
What helps
Pick one action you can finish today, like reviewing five questions from the quiz.

    Planning Rules

    Planning rules that keep the flame steady

    ◎

    Make goals small and clear

    Instead of “study harder,” try “answer 10 practice problems every Monday, Wednesday, and Friday.”

    ✧

    Remember your “why”

    Write the reason behind your goal and keep it where you can see it — your notebook cover or phone wallpaper.

    ≋

    Plan for the strong winds

    Expect busy weeks. Decide in advance what a “minimum day” looks like, like 10 minutes of review.

    ♧

    Find study buddies

    Share your goal with a friend or group. Checking in with each other makes it easier to continue.

    ✓

    Progress over perfection

    Missing one day is normal. What matters is coming back the next day instead of giving up the whole goal.

    ↻

    Reflect and adjust

    Every week, ask yourself what worked and what got in the way. Use your answers to adjust the plan and keep working toward your goal.

    About Our Research

    This website was developed as an output of the research study entitled “Ningas Kugon Cycle: Senior High School Students' Lived Experiences of Initiating and Abandoning Academic Goals”. It aims to raise awareness about the challenges students face in maintaining motivation toward their academic goals and encourage them to develop consistency, persistence, and responsible habits.
